🌍 Kepler's Orbits

Explore elliptical, parabolic, and hyperbolic orbits around a central mass.

Overview

Kepler's laws of planetary motion describe how objects orbit under gravity. This simulation uses the universal variable formulation of Kepler's equation — a single analytic method that works for elliptical (e < 1), parabolic (e = 1), and hyperbolic (e > 1) orbits without needing to switch between methods.

The initial velocity is set via the vis-viva equation: \(|v_0| = \sqrt{\mu (1 + e) / r_0}\), applied tangentially to the position vector. This means you control the orbit shape purely through the eccentricity parameter.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand how eccentricity determines orbit shape (circular → elliptical → parabolic → hyperbolic)
  • Explore the relationship between velocity, distance, and orbital energy (vis-viva)
  • Compare center-of-mass vs. perifocal reference frames
  • Observe conservation of specific energy and angular momentum
  • Identify periapsis, apoapsis, and semi-major axis on live orbits

Controls

  • Space — Play / Pause
  • R — Reset simulation
  • S — Step one frame
  • F — Fit 2D view to screen
  • Scroll — Zoom
  • Drag on canvas — Pan
  • Eccentricity slider has non-linear mapping for fine control at low eccentricities

Features

  • Adjustable masses, gravitational constant, and time step
  • Two reference frames: Center-of-mass and Perifocal
  • Real-time data: eccentricity, specific energy, semi-major axis
  • Orbital trails with history (up to 10,000 points)
  • Auto-scaling viewport that fits the full orbit
