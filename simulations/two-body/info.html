⭐ Three-Body Problem

Simulate the chaotic gravitational interactions of three celestial bodies.

Overview

The three-body problem describes the motion of three point masses under their mutual gravitational attraction. Unlike the two-body problem, which has a closed-form analytic solution (Kepler's laws), the general three-body problem has no known analytic solution — it is inherently chaotic. Small changes in initial conditions lead to dramatically different outcomes.

This simulation solves the equations of motion numerically using a 4th-order Runge-Kutta integrator (RK4), which provides excellent accuracy for short to medium time scales.

Learning Objectives

  • Observe chaotic orbital dynamics and sensitivity to initial conditions
  • Explore how mass ratios affect system behavior and stability
  • Visualize the center of mass and conservation of angular momentum
  • Discover special solutions like the Figure-8 orbit
  • Understand the difference between integrable (2-body) and non-integrable (3-body) systems

Controls

  • Space — Play / Pause
  • R — Reset simulation
  • S — Step one frame
  • F — Zoom to fit
  • + / - — Zoom in / out
  • Drag any body to reposition it
  • Scroll to zoom in/out on the canvas

Key Features

  • Click any body to select it — the camera follows and its position/velocity vectors are displayed
  • Right-click the canvas to add new bodies with zero initial velocity
  • Drag any body to reposition it while paused
  • Adjust individual masses with per-body sliders
